### b. Details of three certifications

**1. CompTIA Security+**

- **Type:** Vendor-neutral.
- **Examination:** One examination.
- **Requirements:** No formal prerequisite; networking and IT knowledge are recommended.
- **Topics:** Threats, vulnerabilities, security architecture, identity and access management, risk management, cryptography, and incident response.
- **Career relevance:** Provides foundational knowledge for security analyst and junior SOC roles.

**2. Cisco Certified CyberOps Associate**

- **Type:** Cisco certification focused on cybersecurity operations.
- **Examination:** One qualifying examination, 200-201 CBROPS.
- **Requirements:** No formal prerequisite.
- **Topics:** Security monitoring, host-based analysis, network intrusion analysis, security operations, and incident response.
- **Career relevance:** Prepares learners for security monitoring and SOC analyst responsibilities.

**3. CISSP — Certified Information Systems Security Professional**

- **Type:** Vendor-neutral.
- **Examination:** One comprehensive examination.
- **Requirements:** Generally five years of cumulative paid work experience across at least two of the eight CISSP domains. Certain qualifications may substitute for up to one year of experience. Candidates without the required experience may qualify as an Associate of ISC2 after passing the examination.
- **Topics:** Security and risk management, asset security, security architecture, network security, identity and access management, security assessment, and security operations.
- **Career relevance:** Particularly relevant to experienced security engineers, security architects, and security managers.

### c. What are the most common qualifications required by employers?

Common qualifications and skills include:

- A degree or HND in Computer Science, Information Technology, Cybersecurity, or a related field.
- Knowledge of networking, TCP/IP, DNS, firewalls, and intrusion detection systems.
- Familiarity with Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) tools such as Splunk and IBM QRadar.
- Knowledge of Linux and Windows operating systems.
- Ability to analyse logs, investigate security alerts, and assist with incident response.
- Understanding of common cyberattacks, vulnerabilities, and security controls.
- Good analytical, communication, documentation, and problem-solving skills.
- Relevant IT or cybersecurity experience, depending on the position.

### d. What industry certifications are required by these employers?

Relevant certifications include:

- CompTIA Security+ — CompTIA.
- CompTIA CySA+ — CompTIA.
- Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) — EC-Council.
- CISSP — ISC2.
- GIAC Security Essentials (GSEC) — GIAC.
- Microsoft Security Operations Analyst (SC-200) — Microsoft.

Certification requirements differ between employers. Some are mandatory, while others are preferred or considered an advantage.

### e. Do any of the certifications match the ones found in Step 1a?

Yes. CompTIA Security+, CEH, CISSP, and GSEC appear in both lists. CompTIA CySA+ and Microsoft SC-200 are additional certifications relevant to security monitoring, threat detection, and incident response.

### f. What kinds of challenges can be found on Google Gruyere?

Google Gruyere is a deliberately vulnerable web application designed to teach web application security and defensive techniques.

Its challenges include:

- Cross-site scripting (XSS).
- Cross-site request forgery (CSRF).
- Injection vulnerabilities.
- Authentication and authorization weaknesses.
- Information disclosure.
- Application logic vulnerabilities.

Other legal cybersecurity training resources include PortSwigger Web Security Academy, TryHackMe, and OverTheWire. These platforms provide exercises for developing cybersecurity skills in authorized training environments.
